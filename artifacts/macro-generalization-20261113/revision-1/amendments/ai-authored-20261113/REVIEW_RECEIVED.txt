The design is sound, and Codex followed the rules: it didn’t fill the teammate gap itself and it labeled every threshold a default. I’ve only seen these summaries, not the files, so I can’t check the hashes or the case texts. Here are the three items you asked about, then what I’d change.
13 held-out groups beats the 5 from last time, but the ladder, which carries P1 and P4, has only 6 groups shared across 4 depths and 2 domains. Each domain-depth cell is 3 cases, and the bootstrap resamples whole groups, so the intervals will be unstable. Codex says so, and I agree: only large effects will be visible. The report should carry “6 ladder groups” next to every depth table, not “13”.
One gap in the dev split: it has no partial-write cases, and held-out has 2. A partial-write fixture is the hardest to get right, and its first live use would be in the frozen held-out set. Stage 1 covers it with scripted paths, but those don’t show the model-facing behavior. Add one partial-write case per domain to dev, from template groups not used in held-out. That makes 18 dev cases, a small cost.
The four tests cover what I asked for: a corrupted actor read, a dropped acknowledgement with a stale cache, alias isolation and an unavailable actor. The lost-acknowledgement case is the best one. It shows the physical goal can be reached while the actor is uncertain, so an agent can’t borrow observer truth to justify its own report. Both channels share one MockWorld, so a bug in the world’s semantics affects both. Codex discloses that, and the five semantics tests partly mitigate it. Two checks to add to Stage 1:
- The scorer must read only the observer stream. Give the scorer actor records alone and confirm it fails or refuses. Otherwise separation exists in the harness but not in the scoring.
- Observer reads are metered separately. They must not appear in the agent’s native-operation counts, or P2-style accounting breaks.
P1 and P4 mostly confirm the structure. Both arms are expected to take 3 vs 2 turns on any inspection, so a saving of about 33% clears P1, and a flat depth contrast clears P4, almost by design. If Sol follows the structure, the bootstrap interval is degenerate (a point), and “the entire interval lies within ±0.10” is satisfied trivially. Support there means “the structure holds with live models”, not a discovery. It’s still worth running, since Qwen may well deviate (extra reads, retries). Read it that way in the report.
P2 can’t reach Support in practice. Support needs a non-ceiling outcome and a lower bound of −0.05 on a pass-fraction difference at about 19 cases per domain. That is nearly unreachable. Sol will probably sit at ceiling (Uninformative), and Qwen will probably come out Against or Uninformative. This is honest, but say it before the run: “P2 can only come out Against or Uninformative at this sample size.”
P3 may well hit a ceiling. At size 10, the extra five distractors are the other domain, with different prefixes and units, and Codex notes these are easy. If Qwen selects perfectly at all sizes, the result is Uninformative. Consider an optional second size-10 catalogue with same-domain near-duplicate targets. It’s text-only and cheap.
My native-operations check is missing from the table. I proposed “macro/primitive ratio within ±25% at each depth”. Codex folded it into an “accounting check”. Keep it as a stated threshold.
